# Brainstorm: a pull or vacuum for bubbles (2026-11-14)

## Problem

Some bubbles can be reached only at a cost: a jump over a gap in the floor, a jump that drops the
player to a lower floor, or a detour. Plus mode should let the player bring bubbles closer without
paying that cost. Beyond that it should make play faster, especially popping trapped enemies and
freeing a partner from a rescue bubble.

Constraints: it has to be quick to use in a fast game (no aiming, no target marker to read); it has
to stay cheap for the planned SNES port (no square roots, few multiplications); it should add as
few new edge cases as possible.

## Who is affected

- Fast players want to clear trapped enemies quickly.
- Chain players want clusters: popping one bubble at a time gives the lowest food tier and no
  EXTEND letters (those need chains of three or more). A tool that pulls single bubbles out of a
  cluster works against them.
- Co-op players: freeing a partner from a rescue bubble is the tensest moment in co-op, and a pull
  could make it quicker.
- Touch and couch players: a fourth action button is a lot, and holding buttons is clumsy on a
  touch screen.

## Background

- Bubbles mostly float above the player, at the ceiling or in corners. A pull limited to the
  facing direction at the player's height would rarely find one.
- Floating bubbles follow the room's airflow: each tile has a direction (up, down, left, right or
  none). The air is read in one place, `level_air_at()` in `src/game/level.c`. Bubbles drift at
  0.35 px per frame and re-read the air only every 16 frames. A partner's rescue bubble follows
  the same air.
- Floating bubbles ignore walls and platforms: `drift()` only keeps them inside the room's outer
  edges.
- `separate()` keeps floating bubbles from overlapping, so bubbles gathered at one spot form a
  touching cluster, which is what chain reactions need.
- The push bubble gave every bubble a speed (`vx`, `vy`) that fades out.
- On the SNES, distances are measured without square roots (`|dx| + |dy|` or a small lookup
  table), and a bubble can home in on a target by stepping on each axis separately.

## Ideas

1. **Forward cone.** Pull the nearest bubble in the half of the room in front of the player, at any
   height. It keeps "the way I'm looking" and finds ceiling bubbles.
2. **Aim with the D-pad.** Up, diagonal or forward picks the zone. Full control. Dropped: too much
   effort in a fast game.
3. **Nearest bubble anywhere.** No direction; the closest bubble comes. Easiest to use.
4. **Target marker.** The bubble that would be pulled blinks. Dropped: nobody looks at it in a fast
   game.
5. **Homing pull.** The pulled bubble follows the player each frame, so pulling mid-jump just works.
6. **Hookshot in the air.** Mid-jump, the player flies to the bubble instead. Dropped: too wild, and
   it competes with the dash.
7. **Pull the whole cluster.** The target brings every bubble touching it, so chains survive.
8. **Vacuum.** No target: bubbles within a radius are drawn towards a point for a while.

## Recommendation

The first recommendation was the forward cone with homing (1 + 5), with the cluster pull (7) as the
upgrade if chains suffered.

The developer then reshaped the vacuum (8) into something better than the rated version: the vacuum
*is* airflow. Pressing the button places a vacuum where the player is. For a short time, the air
inside a circle around it points to its centre instead of the room's air, and then the room's air
takes over again. This needs no new kind of movement and no grid: the air lookup only has to answer
differently inside an active vacuum. It gathers bubbles into a cluster at the centre, which keeps
chains alive. Standing still, it acts as a pull to the player; mid-jump, it gathers bubbles where
the jump started. A partner's rescue bubble follows the air, so it gets drawn in too.

Facts that shaped it: walls do not stop bubbles today, so "the wall still blocks the bubble" needs
an extra check; and normal drift is far too slow for a vacuum (about 2 s for 40 px), so bubbles
inside a vacuum need their own, faster speed and must re-read it every frame.

## Decision

Build a vacuum MVP:

- East face button (Xbox B, PlayStation ○; SNES A). Plus mode only. No energy cost, no cooldown.
  One vacuum per player; pressing again moves it to the new spot.
- Placed at the player's position when pressed, and it stays there (mid-jump: at the jump point).
- A circle with a radius of 5 tiles (40 px), active for 1.5 s (90 frames).
- Inside, the air points to the centre along the axis with the bigger distance, and bubbles follow
  it every frame, faster the closer they get (about 1 px per frame at the edge, about 3 at the
  centre). At the centre they stay put. Where two vacuums overlap, the nearer centre wins.
- It moves every floating bubble (empty, with an enemy, special, letters) and rescue bubbles. It
  does not move enemies or items. A bubble still sliding from a push keeps its push. Nothing pops
  by itself.
- A small swirl sprite marks the centre while the vacuum is active.
- Walls: bubbles pass through them by default. A developer switch makes walls block bubbles inside
  a vacuum, so both versions can be compared in play.
- Cooldown, limits and energy cost get their own brainstorm if the MVP feels good.

## Result after playing the MVP (2026-11-14)

The vacuum felt good, maybe too strong. The biggest problem was visual: the swirl sprite at the
centre was mostly hidden behind the player, and nothing showed how far the vacuum reached.

The swirl was replaced by the SNES way of drawing an area: a window (a left and right edge per
scanline, an HDMA table on the real hardware) with colour math inside it. The game produces up to
two such windows per frame, one per player's vacuum (`game_windows()` in `src/game/game.h`); the PC
platform draws each span as a one-pixel-high rectangle with additive blending, between the
background layer and the sprites, so only the room and the backdrop are tinted. It uses no sprites
and no tiles. The same half-width table decides which bubbles are pulled, so the glow shows exactly
the area that pulls.

The look is a soft blue glow that pulses gently and fades out over the last half second. A darker
circle was the first idea, but the room background is black, and subtracting from black shows
nothing; on the SNES the backdrop can take part in colour math, so adding a colour stays faithful.

## Open questions

- Is it too strong? Cooldown, limits or an energy cost are the next brainstorm.

- Should walls block bubbles in a vacuum? To be decided by playing both versions.
- Faster near the centre, or faster far away? The MVP is faster near the centre; the opposite would
  form the cluster more gently.
- Cooldown, limits, energy cost.
- Does the CPU partner use it?
